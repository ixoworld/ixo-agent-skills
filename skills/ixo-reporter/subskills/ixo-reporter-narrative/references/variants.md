# Narrative variants

Headings below are suggestions in English; write them in the reader's language. Each plan lists sections in order. Drop a section when the certificate has nothing for it, but keep "What is not confirmed": readers rely on it.

## briefing

For a funder or board that will act on this. Short sentences, result first, no scene-setting.

1. **Result.** One or more `check` statements: what Reporter found and when, with the lead if there is one, and what that means for this reader.
2. **What the certificate states.** One `signed` statement per headline figure, in the order the certificate gives them.
3. **What it means.** One to three `reading` statements tied to the audience's decision: reach, scale, timing. Cite the facts.
4. **What is not confirmed.** `check` statements for groups that did not pass, plus `missing` statements for obvious questions the certificate does not answer: who evaluated it, how figures were measured, cost.
5. **Next steps.** `reading` statements such as "Ask the issuer how the gains were measured" or "Check the live status before payment".

## impact-story

For a community or the press. Warmer, but still exact. No invented people, quotes or scenes: if the certificate names no beneficiary, do not create one.

1. **Opening.** A `check` statement that sets trust: "IXO Reporter checked this record on {CHECKED} and found it genuine".
2. **What happened.** `signed` statements woven into prose: what was achieved, where and when, using only facts.
3. **Why it matters.** `reading` statements about what the figures suggest for the people served. Say plainly that this is interpretation.
4. **What we do not know.** `missing` statements, written kindly: "The certificate does not say who carried out the evaluation."
5. **How to check it yourself.** A `check` statement inviting the reader to open the link; the renderer adds the link itself.

For the press, add one early `reading` statement explaining what an outcome certificate is: a signed record of a result, which Reporter checks for authenticity, not truth.

## funder-report

For a payment or due-diligence decision. Formal and complete.

1. **Verification summary.** `check` statements for the result and for each group that did not pass. If the bundle has a lead, convey it in full, first.
2. **The claim.** `signed` statements: every figure the certificate states, and the date its figures are as at (`{ASAT}`) when it has one.
3. **Basis for decision.** `reading` statements linking figures to typical funding questions: delivered or not, scale, period covered. No derived ratios.
4. **Gaps and risks.** `missing` statements for evaluation method, cost, attribution, and anything a funder normally asks that is not recorded. Add `check` statements where Reporter did not run a check, such as evidence files.
5. **Conditions to consider.** `reading` statements proposing what to ask the issuer before paying.

## Tone by audience

| Audience | Voice | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| funder | Precise, decision-oriented | Advocacy, adjectives like "remarkable" |
| board | Brief, plain, one idea per sentence | Detail beyond what a decision needs |
| community | Direct, respectful, "you" and "your" where natural | Funding jargon, acronyms |
| press | Neutral, explanatory, attributed ("the certificate states") | Treating the certificate as proof that statements are true |
