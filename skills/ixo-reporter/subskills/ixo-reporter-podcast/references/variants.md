# Podcast variants

Running orders are for the default length. Scale each part for `short` or `long`; keep the order.

## deep-dive (two hosts)

1. **Cold open, first 20 seconds.** Host A says what Reporter found and when (`check`), including the lead if there is one, and for an example that it is a synthetic example (cite `sample`). Host B names the outcome in a line (`reading` citing facts).
2. **What the certificate states.** Hosts take turns with the figures (`signed`), one figure per turn, each with its unit.
3. **What it means.** A back-and-forth of `reading` statements for the audience. One host asks the question a listener would ask; the other answers from the facts.
4. **What we can't tell.** `missing` and `check` statements: evaluator, method, cost, evidence files not opened.
5. **Close.** For a real certificate, where to check it themselves (reporter.ixo.world, the QR code in the show notes); for an example, that it was a sample. Then the result once more.

## brief (one voice)

1. The result and date (`check`, with the sample notice for examples), then the outcome in a line.
2. The most important figures (`signed`), each with a sentence on what it covers.
3. A few sentences of interpretation (`reading`) and of what is not recorded (`missing`).
4. Where to check it (real certificates), or that it was a sample.

A brief is usually `short`, about three minutes (450 words). With few figures it may run shorter; that is better than padding.

## critique (host and reviewer)

The reviewer's job is to test the certificate, not to attack the programme.

1. The host states the result (`check`). The reviewer says what that result does and does not mean: authentic and unchanged, not proven true.
2. The host presents each figure (`signed`). The reviewer asks what it rests on and answers from the bundle: `check` for what Reporter confirmed, `missing` for what is not recorded.
3. The reviewer's reading of the strongest and weakest points (`reading`, citing facts and groups).
4. The questions a funder should ask the issuer (`reading`).
5. Close with the result and where to check it.

## Speaking guidance

- Say the unit with every figure. Never round, and never turn a figure into a percentage.
- Identifiers such as issuer identities are not read aloud; Reporter puts them in the show notes.
- Avoid protocol words (DID, VC, revoked, snapshot): say identity, signed certificate, withdrawn, figures as at.
- Greetings, questions to the other host and the sign-off are `framing`: short, uncited and never a claim.
- Say each point once. A certificate with a few figures makes a short episode; do not repeat the result or the gaps to fill time.
- No music cues, sound effects or invented guests. If the reader wants production elements, the voice tool adds them without changing words.
