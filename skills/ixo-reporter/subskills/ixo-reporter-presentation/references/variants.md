# Presentation variants

Reporter adds slide 0 (the title slide, with seal, result, date, link and QR code) and the final sources slide. The plans below are the content slides in between. Titles are suggestions; write them in the reader's language.

## detailed (default: about 8 content slides)

1. **The result.** A `check` statement: what Reporter found, when, and the lead if there is one, citing the group that did not pass. A second `check` statement for anything not confirmed.
2. **The outcome.** A `reading` summary of what the certificate is about, citing its facts. Reporter shows the certificate's own title in the sources; do not quote issuer text as if Reporter had checked its truth.
3. **Headline figure.** One `signed` statement with the most important figure.
4. **Other figures.** One `signed` statement per figure, at most four per slide.
5. **Who and when.** `signed` statements for facts that name people, places or dates; otherwise `missing`. Reporter lists the issuer's identity on the sources slide itself.
6. **What it means.** Two or three `reading` statements for the audience.
7. **What is not confirmed.** `check` statements for groups that did not pass, and `missing` statements for method, evaluator and cost.
8. **Check it yourself.** For a real certificate, a `check` statement inviting the audience to scan the QR code on the title slide or open the link. For a synthetic example, which has neither, say it is a sample instead (a `check` statement citing `sample`).

For `short`, merge 2 and 3, drop 5, and merge 7 and 8. For `long`, give each figure its own slide and add a slide of questions to ask the issuer (as `reading` statements).

## presenter (default: about 8 content slides)

Same order, but each slide holds at most two short lines (up to about eight words each) and puts the explanation in `notes`. Notes are statements too, with the same rules. They are what the speaker says, so write them to be spoken.

## Slide text rules

- At most five bullets on a slide, and one sentence per bullet.
- Never put a figure on a slide without its fact id: the renderer shows `[F3]` beside it.
- Do not use slide titles that assert more than the statements show. "Programme delivered" needs a `signed` fact that says so; otherwise use "What the certificate states".
